Jan to May 2026, Computer Architecture (CMPS 3750), team of three

AI Smart Stick

What if a walking stick could see?

A Raspberry Pi 4 running YOLOv8 looks ahead and tells a visually impaired user what’s there through Bluetooth earbuds: “Person, 3 meters ahead.” “Chair on your right.” An ultrasonic sensor catches what the camera misses and the handle vibrates harder the closer you get.

I designed the enclosure from scratch in Fusion 360 and printed it as three modular sections that clip onto a standard stick. It took four prototypes to get the fit right. I also worked on the caregiver dashboard: a live detection feed, danger alerts, GPS on a Leaflet map, and detection analytics, streamed with Server-Sent Events from a Node.js and Express backend, with Flask and SQLite on the Pi.

Detection
80+ object classes, 1 to 2 per second
Haptics
Single pulse under 2 m, double under 1 m, continuous under 50 cm
Hardware cost
Under $200, battery powered

Brixel

A whiteboard, readable without sight, in under three seconds.

Around 19 million visually impaired children can’t see what their teacher writes on the board, and a human aide can cost $25,000 or more a year. Brixel sits on the desk and does the work passively: a Jetson Nano with a webcam captures the board, Google Cloud Vision extracts the text, and Supabase pushes it to a React dashboard that reads it aloud and converts it to Grade 2 Braille.

We shipped the full pipeline at SELU’s first hackathon: hardware with an ultrasonic sensor and haptic feedback, OCR from camera to cloud, a real-time dashboard, browser text-to-speech, Braille Unicode, and printable BRF files for Braille embossers.

Latency
Board to speech and Braille in under 3 seconds
Student effort
None. It runs on its own
Hardware cost
Under $200
